iPhone battery health: when should you replace the battery?

Short answer: when Maximum Capacity is around 80% and the phone no longer gets you through a normal day, it is time to seriously consider a battery replacement. 80% is not a cliff where the battery suddenly fails. It is Apple’s design target: batteries of iPhone 14 models and earlier are designed to retain 80% of their original capacity at 500 complete charge cycles under ideal conditions, and iPhone 15 models at 1000 cycles. Whether you should replace yours depends on Maximum Capacity, cycle count, unexpected shutdowns and how you actually use the phone.

What the 80% number actually means

The Maximum Capacity figure on the Battery Health screen compares your battery’s current capacity with when it was new. Lithium-ion batteries lose capacity as they chemically age. Every rechargeable battery does this, so a falling number does not mean your phone is defective.

Apple’s support documentation states:

  • iPhone 14 models and earlier: designed to retain 80% of original capacity at 500 complete charge cycles under ideal conditions.
  • iPhone 15 models: designed to retain 80% at 1000 complete charge cycles under ideal conditions.
  • For all models, the exact percentage depends on how the device is regularly used and charged.

So 80% is better read as a sign that the battery has reached the end of its designed service window, not a hard failure point. You can keep using a battery below 80%, but each charge lasts noticeably less, and at a low state of charge or in cold weather you are more likely to see performance management or unexpected shutdowns.

How is a charge cycle counted? Plugging in once is not one cycle. According to Apple, you complete one cycle when you have used an amount of energy equal to 100% of the battery’s capacity. Use 60% today and 40% tomorrow and together that is one cycle.

Replace the battery or upgrade the phone? Five steps

  1. Check Maximum Capacity and cycle count. Settings > Battery > Battery Health shows Maximum Capacity; on iPhone 15 models and later you can also see the cycle count in Settings > General > About. Compare it with the 500 or 1000 cycle design target.
  2. Read the Peak Performance Capability section. If it says the phone experienced an unexpected shutdown and performance management has been applied, the battery is struggling to deliver peak power, and a replacement tends to help the most.
  3. Check how long iOS will support your model. If your phone is close to its last major iOS update, a new battery extends its life, but security updates and app compatibility become the next limit.
  4. Check everything else. If storage, screen and cameras are fine and only the battery has aged, a battery replacement is an efficient way to extend the phone’s life.
  5. Decide how long you will keep it. Keeping it another year or more usually justifies a replacement. If you are upgrading within a few months anyway, charging habits and a power bank can carry you.

Use Apple or an Apple Authorized Service Provider for the replacement. A non-genuine battery can trigger an “Unable to verify” message, and the health details shown may not be accurate.

A practical tip before you decide: write down your Maximum Capacity and cycle count today, then check again in a month. If capacity is dropping by a point or more every few weeks, the battery is aging quickly and replacing it sooner rather than later makes sense. If it has barely moved, you probably have more time than you think, especially if you also turn on a charge limit.

Charging habits that extend battery lifespan (per Apple)

  • iPhone 15 models and later: set a Charge Limit. In Settings > Battery > Charging you can choose a limit from 80% to 100% in 5% steps. The phone charges to within a few points of the limit and stops, resuming only if the level drops more than 5% while connected. With a limit below 100%, the phone will occasionally charge to 100% to keep its state-of-charge estimate accurate, which is expected.
  • Optimized Battery Charging. Using on-device machine learning, your iPhone learns your daily routine and, when it predicts you will stay plugged in for a long time, delays charging past 80% so it spends less time full. On iPhone 14 models and earlier it is in Settings > Battery > Battery Health & Charging; on iPhone 15 models and later it is available when Charge Limit is 100%. Apple notes it needs at least 14 days to learn your habits.
  • Avoid heat. Do not charge or leave your iPhone in hot places, including direct sun or a car dashboard in summer, for long periods.
  • Store it half charged. For a spare phone or long-term storage, Apple recommends keeping it about half charged.
  • No need to drain to 0%. Lithium-ion batteries have no memory effect; top up whenever it is convenient.

Frequently asked questions

Should I replace my iPhone battery at 85%?

Usually not yet. At 85% the battery holds about 85% of its original charge, which still covers a typical day for most people. Consider replacing it if you cannot get through the day or have had unexpected shutdowns.

Do iPhone 15 batteries last longer?

Apple says batteries of iPhone 15 models are designed to retain 80% of original capacity at 1000 complete charge cycles under ideal conditions, compared with 500 cycles for iPhone 14 models and earlier. Real results depend on use and charging.

My Maximum Capacity dropped several points at once. Is that normal?

Maximum Capacity is an estimate and can move a little. iOS 14.5 also recalibrated battery health reporting on iPhone 11 models. A large, sudden drop together with heat or shutdowns is worth having checked.

Does an 80% Charge Limit leave me short on battery?

You give up some usable charge in exchange for slower aging. If you need a full battery for a long day, switch the limit to 100% temporarily and set it back later.
