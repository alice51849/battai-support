iPhone battery cycle count: what is normal for your phone’s age?

Short answer: a cycle count is only “high” compared with the battery’s design target. Apple says batteries of iPhone 14 models and earlier are designed to retain 80% of their original capacity at 500 complete charge cycles under ideal conditions, and iPhone 15 models at 1000 (check Apple’s page for newer models). Divide your cycle count by that target, then read it together with Maximum Capacity and the phone’s age. This page shows how.

How a cycle is counted

One charge cycle is not one plug-in. According to Apple, you complete a cycle when you have used an amount of energy equal to 100% of the battery’s capacity, in total. Use 60% today, recharge, and use 40% tomorrow, and together that is one cycle. Apple also notes that a complete charge cycle is normalised between 80% and 100% of original capacity, to account for capacity loss over time.

That means the number mostly reflects how much you use the phone, not how often you top it up. Topping up at your desk several times a day does not multiply your cycle count.

Is my cycle count normal? A quick guide

Work out what share of the design target you have used. This is simple arithmetic against Apple’s published targets, not an Apple grading.

A rough rate check: if you drain about one full battery a day, you add roughly one cycle a day, so a few hundred a year. A count far above that for the phone’s age suggests very heavy use; a count far below suggests light use.

Cycles are only half the story. Batteries also age chemically with time and heat. A phone that is often hot, or stored fully charged for long periods, can lose capacity with a low cycle count. That is why it helps to read cycle count, Maximum Capacity and age together.

Finding the number on newer and older iPhones

  • iPhone 15 models and later: go to Settings > General > About and look for the battery section, which shows the cycle count. Exactly what appears can vary by iOS version and model.
  • iPhone 14 models and earlier: Settings usually does not show the cycle count directly. Apple or an Apple Authorised Service Provider can run a diagnostic. If iPhone Analytics sharing is turned on, the analytics files in Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements > Analytics Data may include it; this is not documented by Apple and the files may not always be there.

Buying or selling a used iPhone

For a second-hand iPhone, ask for three things together: Maximum Capacity (Settings > Battery > Battery Health), the cycle count, and the model. A battery at 88% with 150 cycles tells a different story from 88% with 900 cycles. If the seller cannot show the cycle count on an older model, Maximum Capacity and any “Service” message are the next best signals.

Frequently asked questions

Is 300 battery cycles a lot for an iPhone?

Not by itself. 300 cycles is 60% of the 500 cycle design target for iPhone 14 models and earlier and 30% of the 1000 cycle target for iPhone 15 models. Look at Maximum Capacity and the phone’s age alongside it.

Does charging to 100% add a cycle every time?

No. Apple counts one cycle when you have used energy equal to 100% of the battery’s capacity in total, so two half discharges add up to roughly one cycle, however many times you plug in.

Can a battery with a low cycle count still be worn?

Yes. Batteries also age chemically with time and heat. A phone kept hot or stored fully charged for long periods can lose capacity with relatively few cycles, so check Maximum Capacity too.
